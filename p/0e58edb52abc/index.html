Closing more deals is not the same as growing: the acquisition trap

Written from the public transcript of one episode of GTM Made Simple, for the team that makes the show. Drafted with AI; a machine checked every quoted line against the public transcript, and the table below shows each check. We never invent quotes or facts: a claim we could not trace to the episode is marked.

A company can add customers every month and still shrink underneath. On a recent episode of GTM Made Simple, the speaker describes this as the acquisition trap: new sales are spent replacing customers who left. The way out, he says, is to find out what your existing customers need next.

The test: would the business survive without this month's deals?

The episode opens with a blunt question. "If you don't close those 10 deals this month in your pipeline, will your business collapse?" If the answer is yes, the speaker says, the company is still in survival mode. His goal is a business "where you're not just surviving, you're thriving", because last month's customers are growing and buying more.

What the trap looks like

He draws on his time at Terminus, which he says grew from a million in the first year to five in the second and 15 in the third. Growth hid a problem. "We were not selling more things to our customers." He walks through the arithmetic: with 100 customers, "when 10 customers were no longer renewing with us, guess what? We had to sell 20 more customers to get back to 100." The next round of churn meant selling 30 more. Sales looked strong while "the business is bleeding on the back side of it."

Up-serve, do not just sell

His answer is a question the show calls the sixth in its go-to-market operating system: how do you up-serve your customers? He is clear on the difference: "It's not just selling or cross-selling, it's up-serving." That starts with knowing what makes each customer successful, then asking what two more things you could help with.

He also names why companies stay stuck. "Most companies, most businesses do not spend time with their customers."

How to break out

The method is short. Find the top three things your customers need to be more successful. "Validate that with 10 more customers and see if the three things that they all said are the same." If they match, "Then go build that." He says it might be a product, a service, more of your time or an event. The shift in mindset is to stop thinking about selling again and instead observe "the needs they really have."
